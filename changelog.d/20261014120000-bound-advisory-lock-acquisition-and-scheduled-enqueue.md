Bound advisory-lock acquisition and scheduled background-job enqueue so a wedged lock holder or a never-settling enqueue surfaces as a reported stall instead of a silent, process-wide outage.

- Advisory locks: `acquireAdvisoryLock`/`withAdvisoryLock` without an explicit `timeoutMs` no longer wait forever (MySQL/MariaDB `GET_LOCK` waited one year). Resolution order is per-call `timeoutMs` → `databaseConfiguration.advisoryLockAcquireTimeoutMs` → framework default 15 minutes (`DEFAULT_ADVISORY_LOCK_ACQUIRE_TIMEOUT_MS`, `src/database/advisory-lock-bound.js`); `null` at either level opts back in to the indefinite wait. A wedged holder previously convoyed every later caller on the same lock name and parked each waiter's pooled connection in MariaDB's `User lock` state, draining the pool and stalling dispatch with nothing logged.
- Background jobs: scheduled/cron enqueues are bounded by `backgroundJobs.scheduledEnqueueTimeoutMs` (default 20 minutes, 5 minutes above the lock bound so the precise `AdvisoryLockTimeoutError` names the stuck lock first). A scheduled enqueue that never settles used to keep its key permanently in `pendingEnqueuesByJobKey`, so every later tick skipped itself, the schedule stopped firing, and `stop()` could never drain. On timeout the attempt rejects, reports `context.stage = "background-jobs-scheduled-enqueue-stall"` on the framework-error channel, and clears the key for the next tick.
